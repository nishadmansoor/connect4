On each turn the AI runs a Monte Carlo Tree Search: it plays out hundreds of possible futures, letting its neural network suggest promising moves (the policy) and judge who's winning (the value). The bars show where that search spent its time; it plays the most-visited column. Everything runs in your browser. Harder levels just search more.

The network alone beats a random player but loses to a 4-move minimax search. Add even 50 simulations of tree search and it wins all 10. The network's job is to make the search smart about where to look, and the search's job is to catch the tactics the network misses. That combination is the core idea of AlphaZero.

Training curve

Loss per training iteration (each = 64 self-play games, then 400 minibatches). Policy loss is cross-entropy against the search's move distribution; value loss is squared error against the final game result. Policy loss creeps back up after ~60 iterations, most likely because the replay buffer is now full of games from a stronger player, whose search targets are harder to predict. Playing strength didn't drop: in checks every 5 iterations it beat the win-or-block bot in 80–100% of games throughout.
